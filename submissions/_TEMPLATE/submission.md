**1. What made you choose this problem?**
=>We just finished 12th, and the coding we know is mostly what we memorized for exams. Now we actually want to learn it properly, but it’s confusing. There are hundreds of free videos, we don’t know where to start or what to do next, and we keep jumping between playlists and losing focus. We built CodeLingo because we wanted something that tells us exactly what to learn today, so we can spend our time coding instead of searching for what to study.


**2. Who is it for, and how would they use it in a normal week?**
=>It’s for school and first-year college students who want to learn programming from scratch but get lost studying alone. In a normal week, a student logs in once a day and picks their language, like Python. They read the day’s short chapter, which takes around 15 to 20 minutes, and then take a quick 3-question quiz. If they get something wrong, they tap “Explain with AI” to understand why in simple words. Finishing a chapter keeps their streak going, so they feel motivated to come back the next day. On weekends they can revise old chapters or learn a bit extra to earn more XP.


**3. If you had another month, what would you build next?**
=>First, we’d finish full chapters and quizzes for all 8 languages, because right now only Python is complete. Second, we’d add a small code editor inside the app, so students can practice what they just learned without leaving the page. Third, we’d use AI to notice the topics a student keeps getting wrong and suggest a short revision before the next chapter. After that, we’d add daily reminders, a friends leaderboard, and explanations in Kannada and Hindi, so more students can learn comfortably in the language they think in.
